###### The Old Physics

# Software was an assembly line of handoffs.

- PM → Designer → Engineer → QA → Marketing — every arrow is a queue
- Each handoff costs a meeting, a ticket, a sprint — and loses intent in translation
- Coordination cost grows faster than headcount (Brooks's Law)

<!--
For decades, building software meant an assembly line. The product manager wrote requirements and waited. The designer mocked screens and waited. Engineering built, QA verified, marketing launched — and every arrow between them was a queue: a meeting, a ticket, a sprint boundary. Worse, the idea degraded at every translation. Coordination cost grew faster than headcount. That was the physics of software. [PAUSE:800]
-->

###### What AI Changed

# AI collapsed the cost of implementation — and with it, the need for handoffs.

- AI agents accelerate coding 10–100x
- The bottleneck moved from building to deciding what to build
- When one person can run the whole loop, the queues disappear

<!--
Then AI collapsed the cost of implementation. Coding agents accelerate the build ten to one hundred x — so the bottleneck moved from building to deciding what to build. ###### The Operating Model

# Two loops, one person.

- The Product Loop decides what to build and verifies it worked — "Right thing?"
- The Builder Loop implements and verifies — "Built right?"
- AI runs the code and the commands; taste and discernment stay human

<!--
Here's the operating model. Two loops. The Product Loop decides what's worth building and verifies it worked — adoption, revenue. The Builder Loop implements it and verifies it's built right. AI runs the code and the commands inside both. But taste and discernment — earned opinion and proven expertise — stay human. The ProductBuilder is the person who runs both loops. [PAUSE:800]
